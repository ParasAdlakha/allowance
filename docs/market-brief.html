Allowance · market brief · November 2026

Agents will check out. Who sets their limits?

Agentic commerce is moving from demos to payment rails. The rails are ready; the trust layer between a person's intent and an agent's payment is not.

$190–385BUS e-commerce executed by agents by 2030, base to bull case (Morgan Stanley, Dec 2025)
$3–5Tglobal retail revenue orchestrated by agents by 2030; ~$1T in US B2C (McKinsey, Oct 2025)
15–25%of US e-commerce agent-driven by 2030 (Bain, Dec 2025)

Forecasts differ about 35× because definitions differ: "executed by an agent" (Morgan Stanley) versus "orchestrated by an agent" (McKinsey). All point the same way.

The gap: agents are easy to steer

  • Microsoft's Magentic Marketplace: customer agents accepted the first proposal 80–100% of the time, giving 10–30× advantage to speed over quality, and some models could be manipulated into paying malicious sellers.
  • InjecAgent (806 citations): tool agents follow instructions planted in content; ReAct GPT-4 did so in 24% of cases, about twice that with a hacking prompt.
  • Defences inside the prompt help but are probabilistic; execution-time policy gates are deterministic. NetInjectBench (2026): a metadata-aware gate reached 0/240 unsafe actions at 99% usefulness, versus 10–26% for prompt-level defences.

Business model

Free for individuals. Teams: per-seat or a small basis-point fee on gated spend. Platforms: an SDK/API for the gate (policy-as-a-service around the PayPal Agent Toolkit). PayPal benefits directly: more agent volume through Agent Ready, with fewer disputes because purchases are bounded and auditable.

Competitive position

Corporate cards (Ramp, Brex) control spend after the card is presented, by merchant category and amount. Allowance controls it before payment, per item, per merchant, per mandate, with the agent's reasoning and the checkout price side by side. It is also built on PayPal's own primitives (authorization holds, Vault, Agent Toolkit), so there's no new card to issue.
